title: Digital Twin and IT Architecture Transformation in Automotive Manufacturing
summary: An enterprise architecture proposal for introducing digital twins at a manufacturer of safety-critical vehicle subsystems, written as an MBA case report.
period: October 2026
role: Author, MBA coursework at IU International University
problem: The case company builds safety-critical subsystems for increasingly autonomous vehicles. Its production and quality systems work, but they are split across separate IT departments with no shared information model. Visibility across the manufacturing process is limited, maintenance and quality control are reactive, and factory technology is poorly connected to enterprise IT.
contribution: I analysed the case, selected the architecture method and standards, designed the target business, data, application and technical architecture, and wrote the transformation roadmap, risk controls and recommendation.
technologies: [Enterprise architecture management, TOGAF ADM, ISO 23247 digital twin framework, Industrial IoT, Hybrid edge and cloud architecture, Data governance]
outcomes:
  - Recommended a phased transformation that starts with one production line, so results are proven before large investment.
  - Set four architecture principles, led by safety before automation speed. Predictive models may not change safety-critical machine settings on their own, and automated recommendations need validated limits, a documented owner and human approval.
  - Defined a five-phase roadmap, from discovery and baseline measurement, through a pilot and a minimum viable digital twin, to validation against the baseline and enterprise scaling.
  - Proposed an Architecture Review Board for key decisions, with production and quality among its members.
  - Tied further investment to verified pilot results such as reduced downtime, improved quality, faster investigations and stronger traceability.

The report applies the TOGAF Architecture Development Method for governance and ISO 23247 for the structure of the manufacturing digital twin. Its central argument is the same one behind my AI case study: introduce new technology in small, measured steps, keep a person accountable for critical decisions, and let evidence decide what is scaled.
